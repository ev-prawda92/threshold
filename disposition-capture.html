Why this is less work, not more

Today

One generic list, shared by every req in the company

Fifteen options, none of them about this job. The recruiter picks the closest one, which is usually “lacks required experience” — a phrase that describes 200 rejections and explains none of them. Nothing downstream can use it.

With the ledger

Four options, in the words of this req, pre-checked

The recruiter has just decided this person doesn’t clear something. Naming it is recall, not analysis. Threshold pre-selects what the résumé suggests, so the common case is confirming a guess rather than answering a question — and a click is less work than typing a sentence.

The escape hatches are the point. “Stronger candidates available” keeps a strong field from reading as a throttling gate. “Other” prevents force-fitting — and a req where “other” dominates has just told you that you are not deciding on the criteria you published, which is a finding worth the subscription on its own.

Three ways it gets in front of the recruiter

01
Week one · no code
Custom rejection reasons, configured per job

Greenhouse and Ashby both let you define rejection reasons on a per-job basis. Parse the req by hand, configure its four gates as the reasons, and the capture works today inside the ATS the team already uses. This is how you test whether recruiters will actually do it — before building anything.

02
Month one · browser extension
An overlay on the ATS page

The panel above, injected at the disposition moment. Adds the pre-checked suggestions, which the native field cannot do, and works across ATSs without waiting for a partnership. It writes back to the ATS’s own rejection field, so nothing lives only in Threshold and nobody has to trust a second system of record.

03
Month three · API
Native integration through the partner ecosystem

Harvest and equivalent APIs to read reqs and candidates and write dispositions back. Cleaner, and it needs a partner listing and a security review — which is why it is third, not first.
